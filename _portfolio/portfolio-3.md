## Inspiration

Throughout my undergraduate studies, I worked on several class projects, but this cognition project remains my favorite. I have always been curious about how the mind processes information and how we are able to focus on certain information while ignoring other things happening around us. I was therefore excited to have the opportunity to explore this topic in my **Practicals in Cognition II** class. In this project, I used pre-existing experimental data collected from students enrolled in PSYC 441/449 to examine how focused attention influences the way we process and remember auditory information.

## Project Summary

Attention allows us to focus on relevant information while filtering out competing information around us. However, when multiple sources of information compete for our attention, our ability to process and remember them may be affected. This project examined how focused attention influences the processing and retention of auditory information.

The study involved data from **320 undergraduate students enrolled in PSYC 441/449 across the University of Ghana's Legon and Accra City campuses**. Participants were divided into four groups and exposed to conditions involving either a single auditory passage or two passages presented simultaneously. Their ability to remember the information was assessed using questions about the passages, allowing performance under single and competing auditory conditions, as well as attended and unattended information, to be compared.

The results showed significant differences among the four groups. Participants generally performed better when focusing on a single source of auditory information than when two sources competed for their attention. Under the dual-task conditions, participants also remembered the information they actively attended to better than the unattended information.

Overall, the findings show that **focused attention plays an important role in how effectively we process and remember information, while dividing attention across competing information can reduce memory performance**.
